Hello, and welcome! I'm Jessica, a Business Intelligence Expert who loves turning messy data into answers people can act on.

I've spent most of my career working with large datasets. Today I build dashboards and analysis for stakeholders around the world, including reporting for some of the most important clients in the business. My dashboards are used globally, by thousands of people across the organisation, so every number on them has to be right and every chart has to be clear at a glance. A big part of my role is deciding what **not** to build: behind every report request there is a question, and sometimes the best answer is an existing report, a single number or a five-minute conversation rather than another dashboard.

> Believe in data, not drama.

## How I got here

Every office has one person who ends up with all the spreadsheets. In mine, that was me. If a task involved Excel, a messy report or a column of numbers that didn't add up, it found its way to my desk, and I quietly loved it. Those tasks taught me how processes really work, and what happens when information arrives too late. That curiosity about *why* the numbers looked the way they did led me into data, one step at a time.

- **Teaching myself the tools.** Online courses in SQL, Tableau, Power BI and DAX, Alteryx, and eventually machine learning in Python and R.
- **Learning to answer questions.** A Level 4 Data Analyst apprenticeship, completed with a Distinction, turned those skills into a discipline: cleaning data, writing queries and turning vague requests into clear questions.
- **Learning to communicate.** Moving into business intelligence taught me that a dashboard is only as good as the decision it supports.
- **Learning to predict.** A three-year Level 6 Data Science degree apprenticeship, completed alongside my job, took me into machine learning, natural language processing and forecasting.

And I haven't stopped. The tools change every year, so I keep learning: new techniques, new libraries and new ways of explaining results to people who don't live in data.

I tell the longer version in [Choosing Your Path in Data Science](/blog/choosing-your-path-in-data-science).

## What I enjoy working on

I'm happiest building things that help people make better decisions: models, dashboards and the pipelines behind them. A few projects that show how I work:

- **[An early warning model for air cargo delays](/project/early-warning-air-cargo-delays/):** spotting risk in the free-text notes staff write, and explaining every prediction with SHAP.
- **[Employee attrition prediction](/project/employee-attrition-ann/):** a neural network, and an honest look at what it should and should not be used for.
- **[Exploring climate change with ARIMA](/project/climate-change-arima-forecasting/):** a century of temperature data, and a forecast tested on years the model had never seen.

Across all of them, I care about the same things: starting with the business question, choosing the simplest method that works, testing results properly, and explaining them so that anyone can follow. Colleagues tend to describe me as calm, precise and reliable with a deadline, and I'll happily take that.

## Where I want to make an impact next

I love travelling, and every trip reminds me how much there is worth protecting: forests, oceans, wildlife and the communities that depend on them.

That's where my dream sits. I follow the projects that use data to change how we see the world: organisations tracking deforestation from satellite images, measuring the plastic in our oceans, or showing that, despite the headlines, many things are getting better. They inspire me, and they're where I want to take my skills next.

My [climate change forecasting project](/project/climate-change-arima-forecasting/) was a first small step, and I'm planning more projects on environmental data. Using data to help protect the planet would be the mission of a lifetime: sustainability, conservation and data for good.
